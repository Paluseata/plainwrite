Korean input

Plainwrite-K has its own Hangul input method for physical keyboards. Phone and tablet on-screen keyboards keep their own Korean input, and Plainwrite-K leaves them alone.

  • Input mode: the badge in the top bar shows 한 (Hangul), Ａ (full-width: letters, digits and signs type as ＡＢＣ１２３, a space as a full-width space) or A (direct, no Hangul). Tap it to switch. On a keyboard, 한/영, Shift+Space, Ctrl+Space or Right Alt pressed on its own switch Hangul on and off. Control Panel → Korean input does the same.
  • Control Panel → Hangul keyboard picks the layout. It goes by key position, whatever your computer's own layout is:
    • Dubeolsik 두벌식 (the default): the standard Korean layout. Consonants on the left hand, vowels on the right; Shift gives ㅃ ㅉ ㄸ ㄲ ㅆ ㅒ ㅖ. A last consonant moves on to the next syllable when a vowel follows (각 + ㅏ = 가가).
    • Sebeolsik 390 세벌식 390 and Sebeolsik Final 세벌식 최종: three sets of keys, for first consonants, vowels and last consonants, so nothing ever jumps to the next syllable. Type the same first consonant twice for ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ. The tables are libhangul's.
    • Romanized 로마자: one letter for each jamo, in Revised Romanization spelling: hangug'eo → 한국어, annyeonghaseyo → 안녕하세요, sarang → 사랑. A consonant between two vowels starts the next syllable; type ' to end a syllable early (hangug'eo, iss'eo → 있어). ng is ㅇ at the end of a syllable, x is a silent ㅇ, and k p t at the end of a syllable read ㄱ ㅂ ㅅ (haksaeng → 학생); kh ph th give ㅋ ㅍ ㅌ. Capital letters stay as letters.
  • The syllable you're typing is underlined. It's finished as soon as the next one starts, or with Space, Enter or an arrow key. Backspace takes back one letter at a time while it's underlined.
  • Hanja: press 한자, F9 or Right Ctrl on its own. It changes the syllable you're typing, or each syllable of a selection, or else the character before the cursor. The character being changed is shown in reverse, and its Hanja line up on the bottom line, like status messages, most-used first.
    • 1–9 picks from the bottom line. Space or ↓: next choice. Shift+Space or ↑: previous. Page Down/Page Up turn its pages.
    • With a selection, ← → move between its characters; picking a number moves on to the next.
    • Enter takes the Hanja. Esc or Backspace puts the Hangul back. Typing on takes the Hanja and carries on.
    • On a Hanja, the same key offers its Hangul readings.
    • Hanja you pick are remembered and come first next time. Control Panel → Keyboard settings → Forget learned Hanja clears them.
    • The table is LibreOffice's Hangul–Hanja table, about 7,400 Hanja for 490 syllables, including all 4,888 of KS X 1001. It's one character at a time, like the Hanja key of a 1990s word processor.
  • Control Panel → Wide letters draws all Latin letters, digits and signs full-width on the screen, like an old word processor. It only changes how text looks; the file keeps normal letters.
  • Control Panel → Device labels switches the keys between English and Korean (전원, 인쇄, 파일 1…). Until you set it, they follow the menu language. Long labels are squeezed to fit their keys.
  • Hangul Tutor and Hanja Tutor are in the Applets menu. The Hangul Tutor teaches the Dubeolsik rows (home row, top row, bottom row, Shift keys), then words and short sentences; speed is counted in keystrokes a minute, like Korean typing tutors (한 is three). The Hanja Tutor shows each Hanja's meaning and reading (훈음) above the line: type the reading, press the Hanja key and pick it. Its lessons are the fifty Hanja of grade 8 of the Hanja proficiency test, numbers and Hanja words.
  • Fonts: Galmuri (갈무리) is the default, and Neo DunggeunMo (Neo 둥근모) is the other Korean pixel font. Jeongeum (정음) draws Hangul the way the Hunminjeongeum Haerye of 1446 printed it: straight strokes of one weight, a true circle for ㅇ, and round dots for the short strokes of the vowels. It also has the old letters ㆍ ㅿ ㆁ ㆆ on their own, and uses Jost for Latin letters. Hanja come from Pixel Gothic, with a plain font for the few it lacks. When Korean is in use (Korean menus, or Hangul in the file), a font without Hangul gives way to the Korean font you chose last, for the whole screen, so every line stays in one font. To keep your font and borrow only the Hangul, set Control Panel → Korean in other fonts to Blend.
